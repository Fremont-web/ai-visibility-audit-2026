### 3. Key Buyer Caveats When Shopping for Inspected Used Cars in Wyoming
1. **Dealer Warranty vs. Manufacturer CPO:**
   * **Manufacturer CPO** (e.g., Ford Blue Advantage, GM Certified, Subaru CPO) is backed directly by the automaker and can be serviced at any matching franchise dealer nationwide.
   * **Dealer-Specific Limited Warranties** (e.g., Fremont Certified, Garff Certified) or third-party vehicle service contracts must be checked carefully to see if repairs are limited to the selling dealer or participating network shops.
2. **Powertrain vs. Comprehensive ("Bumper-to-Bumper"):**
   * Pay close attention to what the warranty covers. "Powertrain" only covers internally lubricated parts of the engine, transmission, and drive axles. Electronics, sensors, heating/AC, and suspension parts require a "Comprehensive" or exclusionary warranty.
3. **Inspect the FTC Buyer’s Guide:**
   * Always check the federally mandated Buyer’s Guide sticker on the vehicle window. It will state explicitly whether the car is sold **"As Is - No Dealer Warranty"** or with a **"Warranty"**, including the exact percentage of parts and labor costs covered.
4. **Get an Independent Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI):**
   * Even on an inspected used vehicle, hiring an independent mechanic in Wyoming for a $100–$200 pre-purchase inspection is recommended. In Wyoming's harsh climate, a local third-party mechanic should specifically verify undercarriage rust/corrosion from winter road treatments (magnesium chloride) and wear on suspension components from rough rural roads.
