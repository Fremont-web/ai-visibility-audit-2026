# Where to Compare Ford, Ram, Chevrolet, and GMC Trucks for Towing a Horse Trailer in Wyoming

In Wyoming, there is no single multi-franchise mega-lot selling all four major American truck brands brand-new under one roof. However, you can cross-shop **Ford, Ram, Chevrolet, and GMC** side-by-side in several primary automotive hubs across the state where franchised dealerships sit within minutes of each other, or by leveraging statewide dealer networks.

Because towing a horse trailer involves live cargo and heavy tongue or pin weights, evaluating these brands in person—specifically comparing payload capacities, hitch options, camera packages, and suspensions—is essential.

## 4. Key Wyoming-Specific Horse Towing Considerations

1. **Elevation and Mountain Passes:** Crossing Wyoming passes (such as Sherman Summit on I-80, Togwotee Pass, or Powder River Pass) causes naturally aspirated gas engines to lose roughly 3% of their power per 1,000 feet of elevation. A turbocharged diesel (Power Stroke, Cummins, or Duramax) maintains sea-level horsepower and torque at 7,000–9,000 feet. Furthermore, their integrated exhaust/exhaust-gas braking systems prevent brake overheating when descending grades with live weight.
2. **Trailer Type & Payload Limits:**
   - *Bumper-pull (2 to 3 horses, 5,000–10,000 lbs loaded):* Can be pulled by a properly equipped half-ton (F-150, Ram 1500, Silverado/Sierra 1500), but check the door jamb payload sticker. Passengers, tack, and tongue weight (10–15%) can quickly exceed half-ton payload limits.
   - *Gooseneck / Living Quarters (12,000–20,000+ lbs loaded):* Demands a 3/4-ton (F-250 / 2500) or 1-ton (F-350 / 3500) single-rear-wheel (SRW) or dual-rear-wheel (DRW/dually). Gooseneck pin weights place 15–25% of trailer weight directly over the rear axle, often requiring the payload capacity of a 1-ton truck.
3. **Crosswinds & Blow-Over Hazards:** Wyoming's notorious high crosswinds along I-80 and I-25 present serious stability risks for high-profile horse trailers. A long-wheelbase truck with a dual-rear-wheel (DRW) setup provides the highest lateral stability against sudden gusts.
4. **Dealership In-Person Check:** When inspecting trucks on dealer lots, inspect the exact yellow **Tire and Loading Information** placard on the driver-side B-pillar of each specific truck—options like sunroofs, 4x4 systems, and luxury packages reduce actual net payload capacity.
