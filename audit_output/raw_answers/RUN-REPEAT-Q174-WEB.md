# Where in Wyoming to Compare Ford, Ram, Chevrolet, and GMC Trucks for Towing a Horse Trailer

When shopping for a truck to pull a horse trailer in Wyoming, you face demands unlike almost anywhere else: high-altitude mountain passes (often 6,000–9,000+ feet), severe crosswinds across open corridors like Interstate 80 and Interstate 25, rough dirt ranch roads, and long distances between service hubs.

Because factory franchise agreements prevent any single dealership from selling brand-new Ford, Ram, Chevrolet, and GMC models on a single lot, you cannot walk into one showroom for all four brand-new brands. However, Wyoming shoppers have several practical ways to cross-shop all four makes either in a single trip or through a unified dealer network.

## 2. Regional Border Alternatives
If you want to view dozens of heavy-duty trims in high volume, two neighboring regional markets serve Wyoming horse owners:
* **Northern Colorado (Fort Collins / Greeley / Loveland):** Roughly 45 to 75 minutes south of Cheyenne on I-25. Houses massive commercial truck plazas and regional horse trailer dealerships.
* **Billings, Montana:** Located just north of the border on I-90. Many ranchers and equestrians in Sheridan, Buffalo, and Cody travel to Billings for high-volume truck inventories and large trailer dealerships like Perry's Trailer Sales.

## 4. Key Recommendations for Wyoming Horse Owners

1. **Half-Ton vs. Heavy-Duty (3/4-Ton / 1-Ton):**
   * A half-ton truck (F-150, Ram 1500, Silverado/Sierra 1500) is adequate **only** for small 1- or 2-horse bumper-pull trailers under 7,500 lbs loaded.
   * For 3+ horse slant-loads, goosenecks, or living-quarters trailers (10,000–20,000+ lbs), a **3/4-ton (2500/F-250) or 1-ton (3500/F-350)** truck is strongly advised. The heavier chassis, upgraded cooling, and robust braking prevent the trailer from pushing the truck in severe Wyoming crosswinds.
2. **Choose Turbo Diesel over Naturally Aspirated Gas for High Elevation:**
   * Naturally aspirated gas engines lose approximately 3% of their horsepower for every 1,000 feet of elevation gain. At 7,000 feet (such as crossing Sherman Summit between Cheyenne and Laramie), a gas engine can lose over 20% of its power. Modern turbo-diesels (Power Stroke, Cummins, Duramax) maintain sea-level sea boost pressure at altitude.
3. **Test Drive on Open Highway:**
   * When visiting Casper or Cheyenne, insist on taking the truck onto open highway stretches (like I-25 or US-20/26) to experience the cab noise, wind buffeting, mirror visibility, and ride stiffness before deciding.
4. **Check Payload Capacity First, Not Just Max Towing:**
   * Gooseneck and living-quarters horse trailers transfer 20% to 25% of their total loaded weight onto the truck's bed as pin weight. Check the tire and loading certification sticker on the driver's door jamb to ensure the truck's payload capacity can handle your trailer's pin weight plus passengers, fuel, and cargo.
